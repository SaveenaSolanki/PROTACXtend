## Part 1: Structured Review

## Summary

The document answers the design-side half of the user's question ("what is required to make/solve a PROTAC, are we covering all") with a 10-stage pipeline table, a color-coded coverage matrix for the local ProtacPilot platform, an honest stub list, and seven prioritized gaps. Its strengths are real: the pipeline table is quantitatively accurate on the claims I spot-checked (eHBD≤2 rule, PEG 54%/alkyl 31% linker statistics, MZ1 TC half-life ≈130 s, K_LPT↔potency r-range 0.76–0.98, VERITAC-2 mPFS 5.0 vs 2.1 / HR 0.57, PROTAC-DB growth trajectory); the real-vs-stub disclosure is unusually candid and matches the code; and the approval facts check out against primary sources.

However, the analysis is a recommendations document whose **two top recommendations are built on over-interpreted citations**, and its coverage question is answered on the capability axis while largely ignoring the failure-mode axis that the source briefs themselves document (neosubstrate toxicity, off-target degradation, resistance). The coverage matrix is mostly honest but leans green in three places (ternary "3-method ensemble," "real nodes," "core covered" degradation). Feasibility language ("code-only additions") overstates what wiring PRosettaC or a generative linker actually costs. The document needs a re-scoped gap-1, an added failure-mode row/risk flag, and corrected prioritization before its recommendations should be executed; the underlying evidence base is sound.

## Strengths

- **[S1] Honest stub inventory is genuinely honest and verified.** Every item in the "Honest stub list" I checked against the code exists exactly as described (`exit_vector_detection` not_run stub; supervisor/safety lambdas; P4ward placeholder SMILES constructor; BindingDB local-TSV-only). This is the document's most valuable section and should be preserved verbatim in any revision.
- **[S2] The clinical-reality framing is accurate and well-sourced.** VEPPANU/vepdegestrant as first FDA-approved PROTAC (2026-05-01), VERITAC-2 ESR1m mPFS 5.0 vs 2.1 mo (HR 0.57), Kymera KT-621 Ph1b STAT6 degradation 94%/98%, Nurix bexobrutideg registrational Ph3 — all confirmed against FDA/Reuters/Arvinas and ClinicalTrials.gov records this run.
- **[S3] Pipeline stage table is quantitatively faithful to the literature.** Spot-checks against the Amgen paper full text confirmed K_LPT↔DC50/AUC correlations (r=0.76/0.79 SMARCA2; r=0.98 BRD4BD2), rate↔α (r=0.67/0.99), MZ1 ≈130 s, and the "stability requirement is series-dependent" nuance in the source brief.
- **[S4] Coverage matrix distinguishes partial/degraded from absent**, and the degradation row correctly flags kdeg and the Zhao DC50 model as unimplemented rather than claiming them.
- **[S5] Traceability.** Every matrix cell and gap cites a numbered source in the briefs, and the briefs carry URLs; the provenance section discloses that verifier/reviewer passes were pending (this review is that pass).

## Weaknesses

Numbered findings; each with a concrete fix. Severity: blocker = must fix before acting on the document's recommendations; major = materially changes conclusions or prioritization; minor = accuracy/citation polish; nit = cosmetic.

- **[F1] BLOCKER — Gap-1 fix (a) misreads the Amgen BSA evidence and conflates α with K_LPT.** Section 3, gap 1: *"add a computed-BSA/α proxy to the ternary node (Amgen: BSA ↔ K_LPT ρ=−0.8 [A5])"*. The Amgen paper (verified full text) reports BSA↔K_LPT ρ=−0.8 — **not** BSA↔α — and that correlation comes from **N=3 compounds (1, 3, 11) within a single SMARCA2–VHL system with identical ligands**, using near-native ternary models validated by crystal-structure overlay, 100k docking decoys, linker-strain filtering, and 120 ns MD per model (paper's Methods, Fig. 7d, and the authors' own statement: *"the comparison between BSA and K_LPT is made within the same ligase-target system"*). Three consequences: (i) the recommended "α proxy" would not deliver what the gap claims (α drives degradation rate; BSA correlates with affinity K_LPT, and the two are explicitly "linked but not equivalent" in the paper); (ii) the ρ=−0.8 is a within-series n=3 statistic, not a general predictor — the paper says the correlation "is not surprising, given that these compounds have the same SMARCA2 and VHL ligands"; (iii) computing BSA from the current ensemble's plausibility-score poses is an extrapolation the Amgen workflow does not license, because their BSA came from MD-relaxed, crystal-validated models. **Fix:** re-scope gap-1(a) to "K_LPT-rank proxy: compute protein–protein + protein–PROTAC BSA from existing P4ward/SE3 poses, labeled with the within-series n=3 caveat and a stated requirement that poses be near-native; state explicitly that α (cooperativity) is currently unpredictable in the platform and that the BSA proxy addresses affinity, not rate." Do not call it an "α proxy."
- **[F2] MAJOR — "PRosettaC (benchmarked > AlphaFold3 on DockQ [B19])" is presented without the benchmark's protocol asymmetries, and "code-only" is wrong.** Section 3 gap-1(b) proposes wiring PRosettaC as a 4th ensemble method, justified by the Sci Rep 2025 benchmark. Verified full text of Schulz et al.: (i) AF3 was given **amino-acid sequences only — no PROTAC ligand, no pocket context** ("Inputs were prepared by concatenating relevant amino acid sequences without template guidance or manual restraints"), while PRosettaC received warhead/E3-ligand structures, bound ligands, and linker SMILES with geometric constraints — an apples-to-oranges comparison; (ii) PRosettaC **failed on 11 of 36 systems** (30%), so the comparison is on 25 successes; (iii) the paper's own language: differences "modest in magnitude," PRosettaC top-ranked in 48% of cases, average ΔDockQ +0.11 vs AF3-Minimal, and *the majority of models from all methods scored below the 0.23 "acceptable" DockQ threshold*; (iv) **Rosetta's energy score correlates poorly or inversely with DockQ** (paper's Fig. S27 finding), so in a real design setting (no crystal to rank against) PRosettaC's good poses are not selectable by its own scoring; (v) the benchmark is skewed (22/36 VHL, 16/36 bromodomains). Separately, wiring PRosettaC is not "code-only": it requires Rosetta binaries (license-gated download), PDB preprocessing, PROTAC ligand parameterization, and hundreds of models per system. **Fix:** (a) rewrite the PRosettaC justification with these five caveats, or better, replace the recommendation with DeepTernary (end-to-end, `predict.py`, released weights [B20/B21]) or a PROTACable-Stage-IV-style score as the 4th method; (b) strike "code-only" from the milestone description and add a feasibility/effort table (binaries, weights, GPU, runtime) per gap; (c) note explicitly that ensemble disagreement is already handled by the human gate and that adding a method whose scoring cannot rank its own outputs without ground truth adds noise unless an interface-aware filter (e.g., MD-ensemble check, as the paper itself recommends) is added.
- **[F3] MAJOR — Neosubstrate/off-target degradation toxicity is absent from the coverage matrix and the gap list.** Brief C's failure-mode #2 (CRBN neosubstrates IKZF1/Aiolos, GSPT1, CK1α, SALL4; IMiD-based PROTAC hematotoxicity; phenyl-glutarimide and dihydrouracil mitigations [C51,C53,C56]) and off-target degradation (warhead promiscuity + neosubstrate effects [C40,C56]) never surface in Section 2's matrix or Section 3's prioritized gaps — even though ~90% of clinical PROTACs are CRBN-recruiting and the pipeline row 9 only mentions "selectivity (TMT proteomics)" as a wet-lab step. For a platform whose E3 library is CRBN-heavy, a knowledge-based neosubstrate-risk flag (E3-ligand identity + warhead similarity → known neosubstrate/off-target lists) is implementable and design-relevant. **Fix:** add a matrix row "Safety/selectivity: neosubstrate & off-target degradation risk" (🔴 absent, required per [A23,A37,C51,C53,C56]) and add a prioritized, low-effort gap: "CRBN-neosubstrate and warhead-promiscuity risk flag (static knowledge table + Morgan-similarity alert), before any CRBN-based recommendation is finalized."
- **[F4] MAJOR — The "are we covering all" answer omits the resistance failure mode and under-specifies the PK-PD/hook-effect gap.** Resistance to degraders via genomic alterations in E3-complex components and CRBN loss/mutation is documented in the source briefs ([A33], [A85]) and is partially wired into the local E3-context engine (per B82), yet the analysis never surfaces it as a design consideration or gap. The hook effect and PK-PD appear only in one matrix row labeled "out of core scope," with no explicit statement that "solve a PROTAC" (a drug) requires PK-PD with hook models ([C43,C44], [A80]) and that the platform deliberately stops before it. **Fix:** (i) add one sentence to the bottom line and a row/flag: "Resistance-aware E3/CRBN assessment (E3-complex component mutational status; CRBN non-essentiality) — partially inside the E3 engine, not surfaced to users"; (ii) change the Clinical/PK-PD row verdict from "Gap (out of core scope)" to "Required for 'solve a PROTAC'; deliberately out of scope — document the handoff (in vivo PK/PD + hook-model fitting) in the experiment-request card (gap 6)."
- **[F5] MAJOR — Next-step prioritization is not feasibility-ordered and misses the cheapest wins.** The milestone "close gaps 1–2 first … highest-leverage, code-only additions" is wrong on feasibility for both: gap-1(b) needs Rosetta + GPU-heavy modeling (see F2); gap 2 (generative linker via Link-INVENT/AIMLinker/SynGlue) needs training data, weights (SynGlue `grover_fixed.pt` is 409 MB and not committable per [B73]), and an evaluation protocol — weeks, not "code-only." Meanwhile gap 4 (tissue-aware E3 via GTEx-derived static snapshot or ELiAH) is genuinely cheap and code-mostly ([B53,B54]; the stated blocker is "no API keys," which a static snapshot bypasses), and a neosubstrate-risk flag (F3) is cheaper still and clinically higher-value. **Fix:** reorder the milestone as: (1) K_LPT-rank BSA proxy with honest caveats (F1) + neosubstrate/off-target risk flag (F3); (2) tissue-aware E3 static snapshot (GTEx median expression per E3, tumor-vs-normal) (was gap 4); (3) degradation kinetics via the Zhao model DC50 ∝ Kd_ternary/(E3·k_ub) [A11] (was gap 5) — this is a closed-form computation on existing outputs and cheaper than generative design; (4) generative linker (was gap 2) with a stated weights/training/eval budget; (5) bRo5 chameleonicity; (6) experiment-request card; PRosettaC/DeepTernary only after an interface-aware selection filter exists.
- **[F6] MINOR — Coverage matrix overstates "real/validated" in three cells.** (a) Row 1a "Live ChEMBL … 90 BRD4 binders, 9 s" — this is a CHANGELOG record; brief B explicitly states live-network behavior was not re-executed this run. Change to "live per changelog 2026-08-XX; not re-verified this run." (b) Orchestration row "🟢 real nodes + human gates" sits next to the stub list showing supervisor/safety are passthrough lambdas; the green is defensible only because of that disclosure — add "(supervisor/safety logic lives in the LLM layer)" inline. (c) Degradation "Core covered" — ρ=0.783 is on a **64-molecule** scaffold-split holdout of PROTAC-DB 3.0 (heterogeneous assay conditions, log-DC50, single E3-agnostic SMILES input); "conformal 92.2%" is calibration of the conformal wrapper (target 90%), not accuracy. Present both numbers with their scope: "ρ=0.783 on 64 held-out PROTAC-DB molecules (scaffold split; heterogeneous assay data); conformal coverage 92.2% vs 90% target." Also note the 15,502-row xlsx vs the PROTAC-DB 3.0 paper's ~9,380-entry count discrepancy (count-method dependent, per [B50/B51]) — say "15,502-row xlsx (PROTAC-DB 3.0, count-method variant of the paper's ~9,380 entries)."
- **[F7] MINOR — Clinical-reality paragraph has two citation/status mismatches.** (a) "Kymera KT-621 (STAT6) in Ph2b with 94% skin/98% blood STAT6 degradation [C10]" — the 94%/98% figures are **Ph1b BroADen (n=22)** data [C10]; Ph2b (BROADEN2) enrollment completed but topline is YE 2026 [C9]. - **[F8] MINOR — "the most complete PROTAC-specific agentic coverage found in the tool survey [B8-finding]" overreaches.** The survey found essentially one other PROTAC-specific agentic artifact (a SKILL.md spec, [B72]); "most complete … found in the survey" is a claim over a tiny, self-selected sample and risks reading as an external benchmark. ## Questions for Authors

- **[Q1]** The BSA↔K_LPT ρ=−0.8 (Amgen Fig. 7d) is computed on N=3 compounds in one ligase–target system. Did the gap-1(a) recommendation intend the proxy for within-series ranking of linker variants of a fixed warhead/E3 pair (the only regime the paper supports), or as a general ternary score? If the latter, what validation set is planned?
- **[Q2]** If PRosettaC is wired as a 4th ensemble method, how will its outputs be selected without ground-truth structures, given the Sci Rep benchmark's own finding that Rosetta energy correlates poorly with DockQ? Is an MD-ensemble or interface-aware filter included in the cost estimate?
- **[Q3]** For the "Core covered" degradation row: is the Chemprop ensemble's training label set restricted to consistent assay conditions (e.g., HiBiT/immunoblot in matched cell lines), or does it pool heterogeneous PROTAC-DB DC50 values? What is the expected ρ when the model is evaluated on a per-assay split rather than the 64-molecule scaffold holdout?
- **[Q4]** The neosubstrate/off-target failure mode is absent from the matrix despite being failure-mode #2 in the source brief C. Was this a deliberate scope cut, and if so, where is the scope boundary documented for the reader?
- **[Q5]** "Code-only additions" for gaps 1–2: which items in the SynGlue generative path and PRosettaC wiring are executable with assets already committed, versus requiring downloads/weights/training? Can the milestone be re-costed in effort, not just code?

## Verdict

Solid, largely honest evidence synthesis on the capability axis — the pipeline table, stub list, and clinical facts survive adversarial checking, and the approval claims are confirmed against primary sources. **Not ready to act on as written**: the two top recommendations rest on over-interpreted citations (BSA↔K_LPT conflated with α; PRosettaC>AF3 without protocol caveats), the feasibility language ("code-only") is inaccurate, and the coverage answer omits failure modes (neosubstrate toxicity, resistance) that the document's own evidence briefs document. These are revision risks, not rejections: fix F1–F5 (re-scope gap 1, add failure-mode rows and the neosubstrate flag, reorder milestones by feasibility, correct the PRosettaC justification, add scope statements for PK-PD/resistance), then the document is a defensible internal roadmap. Confidence in the factual core: high (independent verification of ~15 claims, one count nit). Confidence in the recommendations as written: low.
